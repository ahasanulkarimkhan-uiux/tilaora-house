Project Overview

The goal was to answer a guest’s first questions: when to stay, how many people are coming, and what rooms cost. The responsive page starts with dates, guests and a visible starting rate, then leads into six room types, contact details and a local stay review.

01

The problem

A first-time guest may arrive ready to check dates and price. The first screen offers both, then moves directly into room comparison. Atmosphere supports the choice later in the page.

Visitor decision

Can we plan this stay?

Dates, party size and a starting rate need a clear path before the guest scrolls.

Design challenge

Keep the path clear on two screens.

Carry date and guest choices from the hero into room comparison, then show a selected-stay summary and contact step on desktop and mobile.

02

Research

The project brief, guest decision sequence and a structural hospitality reference informed the room comparison and content hierarchy.

01

Task evidence

The guest starts with dates, party size and listed rates, then compares fitting room types and reviews the selected room, adds contact details and sees a subtotal.

02

Content needed

Capacity, bed, defining feature and nightly rate appear together so the first comparison does not depend on opening a new page.

03

Reference role

A small hospitality-room listing informed the structure of the comparison, not Tilaora's facts, visual identity or guest behavior.

Source materials: project brief, journey map, design critique and the current landing page.

A focused sequence

Put planning before storytelling.

The hero search filters room types by capacity and carries dates and guests forward. “Book now” opens a selected-stay summary and contact step; the next screen reviews the subtotal. No live availability is checked.

05 / Outcomes

Key Design Decisions & Outcomes

Three connected choices shape the finished landing page experience.

01

Planning From the First Screen

The hero shows a starting rate and lets guests enter dates and party size before exploring rooms. This is a design outcome, not a measured conversion result.

02

Scannable 6-Room Comparison Grid

The search filters showcased rooms by capacity; each visible card presents bed type, defining feature and listed nightly rate.

03

Connected Local Stay Review

Dates and guests carry from the hero; a selected-stay summary and contact step lead into a subtotal review. The prototype does not check live availability or reserve a room.

06 / Final solution

The complete landing page, across two screens.

The same HTML page adapts from desktop room comparison to a single-column mobile journey. A minimal browser view and three phone frames show the design in context.

Tilaora House is a self-initiated hospitality concept set in Sreemangal. Its room details, rates and generated imagery were created for this portfolio presentation. The responsive page brings date and guest planning, six-room comparison, a selected-stay contact step, later storytelling and a local review into one journey. It does not check live availability or complete a reservation.
